How manpower support works

Organised around the requirement, not held as a standing pool

Manpower support is organised according to customer-specific requirements and project scope. The trade, the qualification, the duration and the location all come from the requirement itself.

Some projects need a manufactured item. Others need people — qualified welders on a shutdown, a fitter for an installation window, a designer to clear a drawing backlog, or NDE personnel to carry out an agreed inspection scope. Those are different problems, and they are scoped differently.

DICE treats a manpower enquiry the same way as any other: the requirement is reviewed technically first, then the appropriate personnel are coordinated against what the work actually needs.

  • Trade and discipline matched to the task
  • Qualifications verified against the stated requirement
  • Duration and location agreed before deployment

What gets settled first

Four things that need an answer before anyone travels

Manpower work goes wrong in predictable ways — unclear scope, unverified qualifications, missing paperwork, or assumptions about what a destination requires. Each gets addressed explicitly.

Project-specific deployment

Personnel are mobilised against a defined scope rather than an open-ended arrangement. The number of people, the trades, the start date and the expected duration are agreed before deployment.

  • Trades and headcount defined
  • Start date and duration agreed
  • Reporting lines and site contact confirmed
  • Timesheet and progress reporting arrangements set

Qualification requirements

The qualification a role actually requires is stated by the customer or derived from the applicable standard, then verified against the individual before deployment.

  • Requirement stated in writing, not inferred
  • Welder qualification matched to process and joint
  • NDE qualification matched to method and level required
  • Validity dates checked against the deployment window

Documentation requirements

Every deployment carries a paperwork requirement, and it varies by customer, site and destination. It is identified at enquiry stage rather than discovered at a gate.

  • Qualification and training records
  • Site induction and access documentation
  • Medical or fitness requirements where the site demands them
  • Records required for the destination, where applicable

Overseas requirements

Work outside the home country introduces obligations that have nothing to do with engineering — and everything to do with whether the deployment is possible at all.

  • Destination-country obligations assessed case by case
  • Qualification recognition checked for the destination
  • Documentation and compliance obligations identified early
  • Feasibility confirmed before anything is committed

A potential area of support

European vendor collaboration

Collaboration with European vendors is a potential area of support that DICE is positioned to explore where a customer requirement calls for it.

The logic is straightforward. Engineering and manufacturing capability in India can support European vendors and projects, and European vendors may need coordinated engineering, fabrication or manpower support that is organised locally and delivered against their standards and documentation requirements.

Whether that is workable for any given project depends entirely on the specifics — the destination, the standards in play, the compliance obligations, and whether suitable capability is available for the programme. Those questions get answered during technical review, not assumed in advance.

A potential area of support — assessed per requirement, not a standing arrangement.

Before any international scope is committed

Five things that have to be assessed

International projects cannot be quoted from a template. Each one has to be assessed against the following, and any of them can change whether the work is viable.

Discuss an international requirement
  • Destination country Where the work or the personnel are going, and what that location requires.
  • Customer requirements Standards, specifications, approvals and reporting the customer expects.
  • Applicable compliance obligations The regulatory and contractual obligations that attach to that destination and scope.
  • Required qualifications and documentation Which qualifications are recognised, and what paperwork must exist before mobilisation.
  • Partner capabilities and availability Whether suitable capability exists and is actually free for the programme.

What this page does not claim

This page describes how manpower and international support would be organised and assessed. It is deliberately explicit about what is not being stated:

  • No overseas deployment, project or contract is claimed as confirmed, completed or in progress.
  • No partnership, vendor relationship or international agreement is claimed as established.
  • No work permits, visas, sponsorship arrangements or immigration approvals are claimed to be held or guaranteed.
  • No overseas offices, branches or registered entities are claimed.
  • No guaranteed international deployment capability is offered. Feasibility is assessed per requirement.
  • No certifications or accreditations are claimed for DICE, partners or personnel.

Personnel availability, qualification validity, compliance obligations and commercial terms are confirmed per project and should be verified in writing before you rely on them.
