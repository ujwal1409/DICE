DICE Coordination hub

Engineering

Design, drafting, 3D modelling, analysis and manufacturing documentation. This is where a requirement becomes something a workshop can actually build from, and where technical review happens before any route is selected.

Typical scope

  • 2D drafting and manufacturing drawings
  • Conceptual design and 3D modelling
  • Jig and fixture design
  • FEA analysis where it is in scope
Engineering & CAD capability

Fabrication

Fabricated assemblies, pipe spools, bends, structural members and fixtures produced to drawing and specification, with the weld and material records the scope calls for. Routing depends on material, size and inspection standard.

Typical scope

  • Process equipment and fabricated assemblies
  • Pipe spools and bends
  • Structural and process equipment
  • NDE scope where applicable
Fabrication capability

Machining

Close-tolerance component manufacture. Tolerance, surface finish, material and quantity decide which machining or cutting route suits the part, which is why those are confirmed during technical review rather than assumed.

Typical scope

  • Precision CNC turning and milling
  • Waterjet cutting
  • Heavy-thickness cutting
  • Specialist machining
Precision Manufacturing capability

Specialist Processes

The processes that turn material and drawings into finished work. Method selection follows the material, geometry and applicable standard, and the personnel carrying it out are qualified for the joint or operation involved.

Typical scope

  • Welding to the applicable standard
  • Laser and plasma profile cutting
  • Pipe cutting and end preparation
  • Induction and cold bends
Specialist Execution capability

Manpower

Where a project needs skilled people rather than a manufactured item. Trades and technical personnel are matched to the discipline, qualification, duration and location the work requires.

Typical scope

  • Welders and fitters
  • Designers
  • NDE personnel
  • Project-specific and overseas support

The operating idea

Local Specialists. International Linkage. One DICE Interface.

Three ideas that describe how the network is meant to work — capability close to the work, reach when a requirement needs it, and a single interface holding it together.

Local Specialists

Much engineering and fabrication work is best done close to where it is needed. Shorter logistics, easier inspection visits, and faster response when something has to be checked in person.

DICE coordinates capability that suits the scope and the location, rather than forcing work through one fixed facility.

International Linkage

Some requirements reach past what is available locally — a process, a size envelope, a material or a trade that has to be sourced further afield.

Where a requirement calls for it, DICE can coordinate cross-border support, subject to the arrangements, approvals and documentation applicable to that particular project.

One DICE Interface

Whatever mix the work ends up needing, the customer keeps one conversation. Enquiry, technical clarification, progress and delivery coordination run through the same point of contact.

Scope is defined once and carried into every downstream activity, so the seams between capabilities stay DICE's problem rather than the customer's.

How capability gets engaged

Selection follows the requirement, every time

There is no standing allocation of work. Each enquiry is reviewed technically first, and the capability is then selected against what that specific scope needs — process, material, tolerance, standard, inspection and schedule.

  • Capability assessed per requirement, not per catalogue
  • Scope, interfaces and responsibilities confirmed before work starts
  • Coordination, documentation and communication stay with DICE

Being precise about it

What depends on the specific project

The network described on this page is a model for how capability is coordinated. Almost everything concrete about it is decided per project, and it is worth being explicit about that.

01

Partner selection

Which capability is engaged depends on the reviewed scope. No supplier holds a standing claim on any category of work.

02

Availability

Capacity is confirmed at enquiry stage against the programme. It is not held open or guaranteed in advance.

03

Scope and interfaces

Boundaries between DICE, the customer and any engaged capability are defined for each project before work starts.

04

Commercial arrangements

Terms, pricing basis and responsibilities are agreed per project. Nothing on this page is a commercial offer.

No claims are made on this page about guaranteed partnerships, approvals, endorsements, accreditations, named vendors, or established international relationships.
