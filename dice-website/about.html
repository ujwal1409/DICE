Company introduction

An engineering and consultancy interface built around the requirement

DICE Engineering & Consultancy brings engineering expertise, specialist partners, manufacturing capability and execution support together behind one point of contact.

Most industrial requirements do not fit neatly inside a single workshop. A project might start as a concept sketch, move through 3D modelling and manufacturing drawings, need plate profiling and CNC machining, then finish as a welded assembly installed on site with qualified trades and inspection documentation. Each of those stages has its own equipment, skills and standards.

DICE exists to hold that chain together. A requirement is received, reviewed technically, and then routed to the capability that genuinely suits it — in-house coordination, a specialist partner facility, a particular manufacturing process, or deployed manpower. The customer keeps one conversation throughout.

Partner-led business model

Coordination rather than ownership of every facility

DICE operates a partner-led model. The company coordinates appropriate specialist capabilities instead of claiming to own every service, machine or facility involved.

This matters in practice. A fixed in-house offering pushes every enquiry down the same route, whether or not that route is right. A coordinated model works the other way around: the requirement is assessed first, and the capability is then selected to match it. Where a specialist facility, process or trade is the better answer, that is what gets engaged.

Responsibility for the customer conversation stays with DICE. Scope definition, technical clarification, progress communication and documentation are coordinated from one place, so the customer is not left managing the seams between suppliers.

The appropriate capability is selected according to the customer’s specific requirement.

How routing decisions are made

To be clear: DICE does not claim ownership of every facility, machine or process shown or described on this website. Services are delivered through a combination of in-house coordination and selected specialist partners, and specific arrangements depend on the project.

One coordinated customer interface

What a single point of contact actually changes

The practical effect of routing a mixed requirement through one coordinated interface instead of several separate suppliers.

01

One conversation, not five

Enquiry, technical clarification, progress updates and delivery coordination all run through the same contact, so context is not lost between parties.

02

Scope defined once

A single scope definition is agreed and then carried into every downstream activity, which reduces the gaps that appear when each supplier interprets a drawing differently.

03

Technical review before routing

Requirements are assessed by engineering first, so the process route, material and standard are considered before anyone quotes or cuts metal.

04

Interfaces owned, not assumed

Handover points between engineering, manufacturing and site activity are identified and agreed rather than left to resolve themselves later.

05

Capacity that can flex

Scope can move between engineering support, manufacturing routes and deployed manpower as a project develops, without restarting the commercial conversation.

06

Documentation kept together

Drawings, revisions, inspection records and handover documentation are coordinated in one place rather than scattered across unrelated suppliers.
